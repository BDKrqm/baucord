baucord

Voice, text and screen sharing on a server you or a friend run. Free, fast, and so lightweight you forget it's there — it sits in the tray using less memory than a browser tab. No accounts, no telemetry, no feed. Just your people, talking.

Download Source on GitHub

Windows / macOS / Linux  ·  ~30–50 MB of RAM  ·  free & open source

What it does

Hang out with a handful of friends: talk, type, share your screen. Nothing you don't need.

voice
Opus over UDP with voice activity detection or push-to-talk. Tuned to keep the gap between someone speaking and you hearing them as short as it can be.
rough connections
Each person gets their own adaptive jitter buffer, so one friend on hotel wi-fi doesn't drag everyone else's audio down with them.
channels
Everyone lands in the Lobby, then splits off — a room for the game, one for the movie, an AFK corner. Lock a channel with a password or cap how many fit. Whoever hosts can edit the list while everyone's connected; the sidebar just updates.
screen share
Share your screen with its audio, 720p or 1080p, up to 60 fps. Show it to just your channel or to the whole server — anyone allowed to watch can hop in and out, in a resizable window with live stats if they're curious.
per-user audio
Right-click anyone to mute them or ride their volume. Settings stick around, so the loud friend stays quiet next time too.
global hotkeys
Mute, deafen and start a screen share from any window. Rebind by pressing the keys you want, not by editing config.
text chat
One shared room for the whole server, whatever channel you're in. Links, images, gifs, emoji — and whoever's mic just died.
your machine
The whole app idles around 30–50 MB of RAM. Closes to the tray and keeps the call running. Settings live in one small file next to the app — no installer, no registry, no background services.
your language
English, Español and Português out of the box, in the app and on this page.
the server
A dumb relay. It forwards packets, that's it — no mixing, no transcoding, no accounts to manage. One small file describes the whole thing, channels included, and it ships in the same zip as the client.

Hosting it is the easy part

There's no company in the middle. One of you runs the server — everyone else just connects to it. No sign-ups, nothing to configure unless you want channels with passwords.

one friend runs the server

On anything that stays on: a spare computer, a cheap rented box, the homelab. Unzip, run it, done.

share the address

That's the invite. There are no accounts to create — a nickname is all anyone needs.

everyone types it once

baucord remembers it. From then on it's open the app, hit connect, start talking.

~/baucord v__VERSION__ 
# on whoever's hosting:
$ unzip baucord-v__VERSION__-linux-x64.zip
$ ./dedicated-server

# everyone else:
$ ./baucord # enter host:port, pick a name

# we have discord at home
$
